## Domain 2: Strategy & Positioning

This is where the company decides what to be in the market. Ideal customer profile, segmentation, messaging architecture, value proposition, competitive counter-positioning, brand voice, pricing narrative. It's also the most agent-resistant of the eight domains. Agents are unusually good research and synthesis partners, but the actual call about what to be, what category to live in, what to attack, what to abandon, is human work. It takes taste, judgment, and the willingness to be wrong publicly.

> *"I believe disagreement about what to position against is the root of most weak positioning."* (April Dunford, [*A guide to advanced B2B positioning*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/a-guide-to-advanced-b2b-positioning), Lenny's Newsletter, Mar 10, 2026)

> *"In an effective strategic narrative, your buyer's enemy is an old mindset that has become a road to ruin."* (Andy Raskin, [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/andyraskin_one-part-of-my-strategic-narrative-framework-activity-7126247639502782464-LtXQ), Oct 2023)

### Why this matters now

April Dunford, who has advised more than 300 B2B tech companies on positioning, says the root of weak positioning is almost always disagreement among the founder, sales, marketing, and product about who the company actually competes with. In an agentic world, this gets worse rather than better, because agents will execute brilliantly against bad positioning and produce ten times the volume of confused messaging.

The structural shift is that with execution velocity unlocked, positioning becomes the bottleneck. Bad positioning used to manifest slowly, over a quarter or two of disappointing pipeline. Now it shows up in two weeks of spam-flagged sender reputation and dropped meetings, because the agents are sending bad messaging at the same speed they would have sent good messaging.

### What strategy and positioning actually involves

Six pieces of work sit inside this domain. They're separate but they all point at the same question: who are we, who are we for, and why would anyone pay us instead of someone else?

**Defining the ideal customer profile and segmenting your accounts.** Who are you actually for, and who are you explicitly *not* for? Most companies skip the second half. The teams that win sort their accounts into tiers (the high-fit accounts you treat like royalty, the medium-fit ones, the long tail) and they decompose each account into the people inside it: the champion who'll push for you, the economic buyer who signs the cheque, the technical evaluator who has to bless it, the end user who'll actually live with it.

**Positioning architecture.** April Dunford's framework is the cleanest version. Five questions: what would your buyer use if you didn't exist (your competitive alternatives), what do you have that those alternatives don't (unique attributes), why does that matter to a buyer (value), who cares most about that value (best-fit customer), and what category do you call yourself (because the category sets the expectations). Skip any of these and your messaging gets fuzzy fast.

**Messaging architecture.** Once positioning is set, you build the actual language: the category narrative, the value props for each persona, adaptations per vertical, an inventory of proof points (case studies, stats, social proof), and a library of how to handle the objections you've heard a thousand times.

**Competitive counter-positioning.** A battlecard for each major competitor. Patterns from won and lost deals. The "reasons to switch" story for buyers already using somebody else. The judgment call about when to attack a competitor publicly and when to ignore them.

**Brand voice codified into something an agent can follow.** Tonal guidelines (are you cinematic, clinical, or conversational?), vocabulary you own and vocabulary you avoid, sentence structure patterns, visual identity rules, and a knowledge base that the Brand Governance Agent uses to decide whether content is on-brand. This is the artifact that lets agents produce hundreds of pieces a week without making everything sound generic.

**Pricing narrative.** Anchor points, packaging logic, discount discipline, the language you use to justify the price. Pricing communicates positioning more loudly than any tagline. A 5× price differential is a category statement whether you intended it as one or not.

### What works in 2026

**Run positioning as a cross-functional exercise, not a marketing one.** April Dunford's core argument is that positioning cannot live in the marketing department. If the CEO, sales lead, and product lead aren't in the room contributing, marketing ends up guessing about what makes the product special and who it's actually for. With agents in the loop, this matters more, not less, because the agents will execute confidently against whatever positioning you give them, including the wrong one.

**Reconcile the competitive set before anything else.** Every function in a B2B company has its own bias about who you compete with. Marketing watches competitors with big media spend. Sales watches who shows up on shortlists. Product watches who's shipping similar features. Founders watch who's raising money. They're often looking at four different lists. Until those lists agree, you don't have positioning.

**Codify your brand voice into something an agent can read.** This is the single highest-leverage strategy move in an agentic stack. A Brand Governance Agent, trained on your highest-performing content, your dictionary of preferred and banned terms, and your tonal guidelines, becomes the gate that prevents the execution layer from shipping garbage. Without it, you produce a hundred times the volume of off-brand content, which is worse than producing nothing.

**Monitor competitors continuously, not on a quarterly schedule.** Agents can watch competitor pricing pages, hiring patterns, and messaging drift every week without human effort. Quarterly competitive refreshes are a leftover from pre-agentic ops; the wind shifts faster than that now.

**Treat pricing as positioning, not a finance exercise.** Pricing tells the market what category you're in more loudly than your tagline does. If you charge $99 a month and your competitors charge $50K a year, you're in different categories regardless of what your homepage says.

### Tactical Playbooks

#### Playbook A. Build a Brand Governance Agent in Claude Projects (8-agent parallel)

**System prompt skeleton:**
```
You are a [voice/grammar/terminology/legal/etc.] auditor for [Brand X].
Read the attached style guide (knowledge file).
For each violation, output: line number | violation type | severity (1-3) | quoted phrase | suggested replacement.
Score the document 0-100 on your single dimension.
Do NOT flag dimensions outside your assigned scope.
```

**Style-guide knowledge structure (uploaded to Project):**
- Voice axes (e.g., "cinematic vs clinical 7/10")
- Vocabulary boundaries (terms-we-own / terms-we-avoid lists)
- Sentence structure patterns (banned: "In today's fast-paced world…")
- Persona table (champion, EB, technical evaluator)
- Failure-mode examples (5-10 annotated bad outputs)

**Run pattern:** orchestrator agent dispatches to 8 specialists in parallel, aggregates scores into a single Brand Governance Score. Threshold: <85 = block publish; 85-94 = human review; ≥95 = auto-publish.

**Reference impl:** [anthropics/skills `brand-guidelines`](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/tree/main/skills/brand-guidelines) + the [Animalz Claude-Code workflow](https://www.theworkflow.digital/p/claude-code-style-guide-animalz).

**Cross-link:** Domain 0 AgentOps Playbook A, same architecture viewed from the operations layer.

#### Playbook B. Dunford's 5-Component Canvas as Fillable Template

| # | Component | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Competitive alternatives | What would the buyer use if we didn't exist? | List (incl. status quo, spreadsheets, in-house builds) |
| 2 | Unique attributes | What do we have that *no* alternative has? | 3-5 features/capabilities |
| 3 | Value | Why does each unique attribute matter to a buyer? | Buyer-language outcomes |
| 4 | Best-fit customer | Who cares *most* about this value? | ICP definition + negative ICP |
| 5 | Market category | What frame makes our value obvious? | Category statement |

Templates: [Miro community](https://miro.com/templates/april-dunfords-positioning-template/), Figma community, April's official PDF (gated form on aprildunford.com).

#### Playbook C. Reconcile Competitive Set Across CEO/Sales/Marketing/Product

90-minute workshop. Each function lists their top 5 competitors *independently* before the meeting. Compare lists in the room. Dunford's finding: **the four lists usually agree on at most 1-2 names.** Force-rank the union, then narrow to a single "official" set. Annual cadence; monthly delta review run by a Klue/Crayon/Clay agent.

### Common Failure Modes

- **Marketing-only positioning.** The CEO and sales lead don't agree, so the messaging contradicts what's pitched in deals.
- **Treating positioning as a deliverable, not a process.** A positioning doc that lives in a slide deck and never updates becomes wrong fast.
- **Following B2C frameworks for B2B work.** Most positioning courses are full of toothpaste and shampoo examples. B2B positioning works differently, multi-stakeholder buying, longer cycles, higher dollar values.
- **Mistaking a tagline for positioning.** Positioning is a market structure decision. A tagline is a sentence.
- **Updating positioning before validating.** Synthetic personas + Wynter testing + sales call analysis should validate every material positioning shift before it ships.

## Frequently asked questions about strategy and positioning

### Why is positioning the most agent-resistant domain?

Agents are extraordinary research and synthesis partners; they can map competitors, surface positioning gaps, and pressure-test narratives against synthetic personas. But the actual call about *what to be in the market* requires taste, judgment, and the willingness to be wrong publicly. That call is irreducibly human work. Agents execute against positioning; they don't decide it.

### What is April Dunford's 5-component canvas?

Five questions every B2B positioning exercise must answer: (1) competitive alternatives, what would the buyer use if you didn't exist? (2) unique attributes, what only you have? (3) value, why does that matter to a buyer? (4) best-fit customer, who cares most? (5) market category, what frame makes your value obvious? Templates available on Miro and Figma. Dunford has worked with 300+ B2B tech companies using this canvas.

### What is a Brand Governance Agent?

A multi-agent system that reviews every public-facing output before publish. The 8-agent parallel architecture (Animalz pattern) runs voice, grammar, banned-terms, legal-claims, persona-fit, sentence-structure, terminology, and factual-grounding auditors simultaneously, then aggregates a 0-100 Brand Governance Score. Threshold: <85 blocks; 85-94 routes to human review; ≥95 auto-publishes. Reference implementation: anthropics/skills brand-guidelines repo.

### Should you reposition or remessage?

Almost always: remessage. April Dunford's March 2026 warning: teams reposition far less often than they think. Repositioning changes the market frame; remessaging changes how you describe yourself within the same frame. Disagreement among CEO/sales/marketing/product on the *competitive set* is the single biggest source of weak positioning; fix that before changing the position itself.
